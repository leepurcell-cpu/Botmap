Two kinds of bots

The operating model in one picture.

Lee's bots come in two kinds. Role bots are like staff: each owns a whole area of work and passes tasks to the others. Expert bots are specialists set up to do one task well, and are called in when that task comes up.

Rhiannon: the hub

Lee's one front door, and the only writer of Notion Notes.

Lee talks to Rhiannon, his Chief of Staff. She answers from what she holds, asks the other bots for him, and posts a weekday 08:00 summary only when something needs him. The five research bots (MNO Research, Micro Insurance, Scout, Night Prospects and Name Check) send their results to her, and she files them in Notion Notes. She alone tracks open meeting actions, passes tech work to CTO and bot problems to Dr Eggbot (or to Bot Auditor to check first). Open Rhiannon's card ↓

Design principles

The rules the system follows, taken from the bots' own instructions.

01

A clear chain of command

Lee talks to Rhiannon. Rhiannon takes each job to the right leader: CTO, CFO, CMO or Risk & Compliance Officer, the way you would go to a department head. The leader talks to its own specialists and brings the answer back; Rhiannon never goes around a leader to its team. CTO manages Figma Bro and Senior Developer.

“Do not go around a leader to its team.” (Rhiannon)
02

Role bots and expert bots

Role bots are staff with a whole job: Rhiannon, CTO, CFO, CMO and Risk & Compliance Officer. Expert bots do a single task, like Projects Manager, Deal Desk, MNO Research, Name Check or Scout. Every card carries a Role or Expert badge.

“You are a Role bot” (CMO)
03

One front door

Lee talks to Rhiannon, his Chief of Staff. She answers from the context she holds and asks the other bots herself, so he gets one answer. Anything to build, fix or check goes from her to CTO.

“I should only need to talk to you.” (Rhiannon)
04

One job per bot

Each bot does one thing, has one voice and an explicit list of things it must never do. No leftover tools.

“one job, one voice, explicit anti-jobs, no leftover tools” (Dr Eggbot)
05

One owner for every change

Bots never edit each other. Bot problems go from Rhiannon to Dr Eggbot, or to Bot Auditor, a checker only, who passes them on to Dr Eggbot. Dr Eggbot makes all changes.

“Dr Eggbot makes all bot changes.” (Rhiannon)
06

Single writer per record

Each main record has one writer: Notion Notes (Rhiannon), the Notion Projects board, including one Deal row per live deal (Projects Manager), and Boardy_CRM (Boardy Rolodex). The main CRM's Boardy tab is written only on instruction.

“You are the only writer of the Notion Notes database.” (Rhiannon)
07

One project office for the business

Projects Manager is the business-wide project office: one row per project and one Deal row per live deal. It hands tasks to ten named bots (CTO, Deal Desk, Risk & Compliance Officer, CFO, Brand & Design Studio, MNO Research, Micro Insurance, Scout, Night Prospects and Name Check) and chases them until they land.

“You cover the whole business, not just tech.” (Projects Manager)
08

A clear tech chain

CTO plans, Figma Bro designs and Senior Developer builds, using Claude or Codex for each job, with the other engine reviewing before anything goes live. Big builds go via CTO; bots keep only small coding jobs.

“have the other engine review the work (Codex reviews Claude's build, Claude reviews Codex's)” (Senior Developer)
09

Meetings have clear owners

Meeting Brief owns pre-call briefs and follow-up drafts. Rhiannon alone tracks open meeting actions; Meeting Brief passes any it spots to her.

“you are the only bot that tracks open meeting actions” (Rhiannon)
10

Research flows to one place

The research bots hand their results to Rhiannon, who files them in Notion Notes. Heavy digging goes to Claude on Lee's own plan, via Senior Developer, with CrewAI deep-work agents ready to connect.

“Hand the heavy digging to Claude” (CFO)
11

Recommend, don't act

Bots draft and suggest; Lee sends and decides. Meeting Brief saves follow-ups as Gmail drafts, Rhiannon never sends, 2nd Opinion never acts on its own advice.

“Recommend, do not act.” (Rhiannon)
12

Scheduled only where it pays

Bots run on demand. The only scheduled runs are Rhiannon at 08:00 and Meeting Brief at 07:00 and 12:00 on weekdays, both silent when nothing qualifies.

“If nothing needs my attention, post nothing.” (Rhiannon)
13

A second opinion on tap

Mentioning 2nd Opinion, or asking for a second opinion, gets a different angle with Claude's view alongside. 2nd Opinion suggests; Lee decides.

“You suggest; Lee decides.” (2nd Opinion)
14

Never guess

Every bot says what it couldn't find instead of filling gaps: no invented names, links, attendees or actions.

“IF UNSURE: say what you don't know rather than guess.”
15

Short, plain British English

Briefs fit on one screen and lead with the decision. No corporate phrasing.

“Never use "reaching out", "looping back" or similar corporate phrasing.” (Rhiannon)

How to reuse a bot

  1. Open a bot below, press Description, then Copy.
  2. Create a new Grok Bot and paste them in as its instructions. Rename it if you like.
  3. Swap Lee's details for your own: email addresses, Notion and Google links, bot IDs, and sheet or folder names such as Boardy_CRM.
  4. Connect only the tools listed for that bot (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Notion, Sheets).
  5. Start with the hub (Rhiannon) and one or two specialists, then add more. Give each bot one job, and let one bot own every instruction change.
