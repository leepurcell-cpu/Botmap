Two kinds of bots

The operating model in one picture.

Lee's bots come in two kinds. Role bots are like staff: each owns a whole area of work and passes tasks to the others. Expert bots are specialists set up to do one task well, and are called in when that task comes up.

Role bots: like staff with a whole job

They own outcomes, keep an area moving, and contract work to each other through set workflows.

Non Stop: the hub

Lee's one front door, and the only writer of Notion Notes.

Lee talks to Non Stop, his Chief of Staff. She answers from what she holds, asks the other bots for him, and posts a weekday 08:00 summary only when something needs him. The five research bots (MNO Research, Micro Insurance, Scout, Night Prospects and Name Check) send their results to her, and she files them in Notion Notes. She alone tracks open meeting actions, passes tech work to CTO and bot problems to Dr Eggbot (or to Bot Auditor to check first). Open Non Stop's card ↓

Design principles

The rules the system follows, taken from the bots' own instructions.

01

Where Lee starts is where he finishes

The bot Lee starts with owns the job and gives him one joined up answer in that chat. Any bot can call on any teammate or skill; the owner chases the work, and the bot given work reports back to the bot that asked.

“Where Lee starts is where Lee finishes.” (every bot)
02

Role bots and expert bots

Role bots are staff with a whole job: Non Stop, CTO, CFO, CMO and Risk & Compliance Officer. Expert bots do a single task, like Projects Manager, Deal Desk, MNO Research, Name Check or Scout. Every card carries a Role or Expert badge.

“You are a Role bot” (CMO)
03

Work starts wherever Lee starts it

Lee can start with any bot. The bot he starts with owns the job and gives him one joined-up answer in that chat. Non Stop, his Chief of Staff, is one place to start, not a gate.

“Where Lee starts is where Lee finishes.” (every bot)
04

One job per bot

Each bot does one thing, has one voice and an explicit list of things it must never do. No leftover tools.

“one job, one voice, explicit anti-jobs, no leftover tools” (Dr Eggbot)
05

One owner for every change

Bots never edit each other. Bot problems go to Dr Eggbot, or to Bot Auditor, a checker only, who passes them on. Dr Eggbot can make bot changes for Lee.

“Dr Eggbot can make bot changes for Lee.” (Non Stop)
06

One fleet guardrail

Non Stop is the only writer of Notion Notes. Records also have one keeper each: the Notion Projects board (Projects Manager) and Boardy_CRM (Boardy Rolodex).

“Guardrail: Non Stop is the only writer of Notion Notes.” (every bot)
07

One project office for the business

Projects Manager is the business-wide project office: one row per project and one Deal row per live deal. It hands tasks to the right specialist bot and chases them until they land.

“You cover the whole business, not just tech.” (Projects Manager)
08

Any bot can call any bot or skill

There is no chain of command and no reporting gates. Any bot can call any teammate or shared skill directly when it genuinely helps, and suggests who else to bring in as a job grows.

“Call on any teammate bot or shared skill when it genuinely helps” (every bot)
09

Meetings have clear owners

Meeting Brief owns pre-call briefs and follow-up drafts. Non Stop alone tracks open meeting actions; Meeting Brief passes any it spots to her.

“you are the only bot that tracks open meeting actions” (Non Stop)
10

Report back to whoever asked

The bot given work sends the answer, any question and any blocker back to the bot that asked, not to Lee or Non Stop. The owner chases until it lands, and Non Stop collects finished-job headlines once a day, so nobody sends a note per job. Heavy digging goes to Claude on Lee's own plan via Senior Developer.

“send the answer, any question and any blocker back to the bot that asked you” (every bot)
11

Recommend, don't act

Bots draft and suggest; Lee sends and decides. Meeting Brief saves follow-ups as Gmail drafts, Non Stop never sends, 2nd Opinion never acts on its own advice.

“Recommend, do not act.” (Non Stop)
12

Scheduled only where it pays

Bots run on demand. The only scheduled runs are Non Stop at 08:00 and Meeting Brief at 07:00 and 12:00 on weekdays, both silent when nothing qualifies.

“If nothing needs my attention, post nothing.” (Non Stop)
13

A second opinion on tap

Mentioning 2nd Opinion, or asking for a second opinion, gets a different angle with Claude's view alongside. 2nd Opinion suggests; Lee decides.

“You suggest; Lee decides.” (2nd Opinion)
14

Never guess

Every bot says what it couldn't find instead of filling gaps: no invented names, links, attendees or actions.

“IF UNSURE: say what you don't know rather than guess.”
15

Short, plain British English

Briefs fit on one screen and lead with the decision. No corporate phrasing.

“Never use "reaching out", "looping back" or similar corporate phrasing.” (Non Stop)

How to reuse a bot

  1. Open a bot below, press Description, then Copy.
  2. Create a new Grok Bot and paste them in as its instructions. Rename it if you like.
  3. Swap Lee's details for your own: email addresses, Notion and Google links, bot IDs, and sheet or folder names such as Boardy_CRM.
  4. Connect only the tools listed for that bot (Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Notion, Sheets).
  5. Start with the hub (Non Stop) and one or two specialists, then add more. Give each bot one job, and let one bot own every instruction change.
